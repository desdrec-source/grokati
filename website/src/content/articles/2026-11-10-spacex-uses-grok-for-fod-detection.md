On 9–10 November 2026, SpaceX VP of Launch Kiko Dontchev (handle @TurkeyBeaver) described Grok being used to identify foreign object debris (FOD) in aerospace parts. Elon Musk quoted the post with the single word “Grok”.

> SpaceX VP of Launch @TurkeyBeaver says Grok is now helping them identify foreign object debris in aerospace parts, and that Grok (99.9%) is better at it than humans (94%).
>
> "We’ve automated being able to detect FOD. We’ll have an inspector go in and borescope a tube, and we find that humans are about 94% perfect (at) finding a piece of it. Grok analyzing the same video is 99.9% perfect."

## What was announced

From the quoted statements:

* An inspector uses a borescope (a camera on a flexible probe) inside a tube.
* Grok analyzes the same video footage and detects foreign object debris.
* On that inspection task, Grok reached 99.9% accuracy compared with about 94% for human inspectors reviewing the same footage.
* The work is described as automated detection of FOD.

## Context

Foreign object debris is a long-standing concern in aerospace because even small fragments (metal shavings, wire clippings, fasteners) can damage engines or other hardware. SpaceX acquired xAI earlier in 2026. The numbers come from comments attributed to the SpaceX VP of Launch at a recent industry summit, as reported by Sawyer Merritt.

## Limits of this report

This article reports only the claims in the X post quoted by Elon Musk. It does not include independent verification of the accuracy figures, details on the dataset size, false-positive rates, which Grok model or version was used, or how the system is integrated into production inspection workflows. Broader SpaceX statements about AI speeding other visual checks (for example on Starlink hardware) were not part of the quoted post.
