On 29 September 2026, **@imagine** posted a partnership case study: two filmmakers used Grok Imagine to make a pilot of Homer’s *Odyssey*, with no cameras and no sets. The studio named in the post is [@WonderStudiosX](https://x.com/WonderStudiosX).

> No cameras. No sets. Just two filmmakers with a mission:
>
> Push @imagine's cinematic capabilities to their limits and make a pilot of Homer’s Odyssey.
>
> @WonderStudiosX … delivered the pilot in 15 days, using 2,070 images and 1,558 videos.

The parent post includes a video. Playback on this page uses the official X embed for [that post](https://x.com/imagine/status/2105021180558413985). Direct `video.twimg.com` links are not used here.

## What was announced

From the @imagine thread on 29 September 2026:

- Wonder Studios delivered the pilot in **15 days**, using **2,070 images** and **1,558 videos**. Those figures are @imagine’s published counts.
- Creative lead named in the thread: **Dev Walker**. A later reply in the same conversation thanks **@glumlot_** for creative direction and says they look forward to making Episode 1 next.
- A follow-up says short Odyssey episodes made with Grok Imagine are “coming soon,” and points readers to [grok.com/imagine](https://grok.com/imagine).

Tips the thread attributes to Walker / the Wonder Studios process:

- **Segmentation:** lock a gritty, textured, slightly desaturated base style, then use segmentation to change clothing, hair, props, and other elements in each image.
- **Speed Mode vs Quality Mode:** Speed Mode is described as the way to get cinematic visuals in volume; Quality Mode as cleaner and more precise for studio and ad-style work. Wonder Studios used Speed Mode to generate a wall of visuals and cherry-pick.
- **Imperfection:** the thread says perfectly clean scenes read as “AI vibes,” and that off-level framing, a subject pushed to the side, and a wobbly camera made the pilot feel human. It tells readers to ask for those imperfections in prompts.
- **Reference + Omni video:** lock an image as a “Reference” to unlock Grok Imagine’s Omni video model, which the post says makes characters more responsive. Wonder Studios did this across **1,400 takes**.
- **Prompt wording:** if a shot keeps drifting, cut words open to interpretation (example given: a character “crossing” a scene) and spell out motion (example: “enters left, exits right”).
